Visual assets — photography, illustration, and iconography — do more than decorate a layout. They communicate the Alchemetryx worldview: data-driven but human, rigorous but warm, professional without being sterile. Every image you choose either reinforces that identity or erodes it. The standards in this section give you a clear, practical framework for making the right decisions, whether you are selecting a hero photograph for a website, commissioning an illustration for a report, or choosing an icon for a dashboard component.

## Photography Style

Alchemetryx photography should feel purposeful and human. The goal is authenticity — images that look like they were captured in real working environments, not assembled in a studio for a generic corporate website. Choose photographs where the subjects convey focus, confidence, and intelligence. People should appear engaged and in motion — thinking, collaborating, presenting, or analysing — rather than posed and performing for the camera.

Lighting should be warm and natural wherever possible. Window light, soft indoor ambience, and natural environments create the approachability that the brand personality demands. Avoid the cold, desaturated, blue-filtered aesthetic that pervades generic corporate photography — it signals distance and impersonality, the opposite of what Alchemetryx stands for.

When sourcing photography for data or strategy contexts, prefer images that show the environment where that work actually happens: a screen filled with meaningful data, a whiteboard covered in frameworks, a conversation over a printed brief. These images ground the brand's positioning in credibility and craft.

## Photography Dos & Don'ts

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="✅ Do" icon="check" href="/brand/imagery-iconography#photography-dos">
    - Use real people in authentic, recognisable working environments — offices, workshops, client spaces.
    - Seek out warm, natural lighting that gives images a human and approachable quality.
    - Ensure diverse and representative portrayal of people across gender, ethnicity, age, and background.
    - Prioritise action and process shots — people doing the work — over static portraiture.
  </Card>
  <Card title="❌ Don't" icon="xmark" href="/brand/imagery-iconography#photography-donts">
    - Use images that feature the generic staged smiles and artificial poses of low-quality stock photography.
    - Select images with cold, blue-tinted colour grading or heavy desaturation that conflicts with the brand's warmth.
    - Use clip-art illustrations, cartoonish graphics, or any imagery style that undercuts professional credibility.
    - Choose images where competing brand colours — reds, purples, bright greens — dominate the frame and clash with the Alchemetryx palette.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Illustration Style

When illustrations are used, they should extend the brand's analytical intelligence rather than soften or infantilise it. Keep illustrations minimal and geometric — flat forms, clean lines, and a limited palette drawn exclusively from the Alchemetryx colour system. No gradients, no textures, no drop shadows, no three-dimensional rendering.

Abstract data visualisation motifs are the natural language of Alchemetryx illustration: flowing graphs, node-and-edge network diagrams, grid structures, data flow paths, and geometric transformations. These visual idioms reinforce the brand's positioning at the intersection of measurement and strategy. They signal rigour without requiring literal depiction of the work.

Avoid figurative or character-based illustration styles — these tend to communicate playfulness more than precision, and they date quickly. The illustration system should feel as timeless as the typeface and colour choices it accompanies.

## Iconography

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Digital Icons">
    Use **Font Awesome 6** in the **solid style** for all digital UI applications — websites, dashboards, apps, and digital documents. Solid icons deliver the weight and legibility necessary for interface use, particularly at small sizes where outlined strokes can become indistinct.

    **Standard icon sizes:**
    | Size | Use |
    |---|---|
    | 16px | Inline text icons, table indicators, compact UI |
    | 20px | Standard UI elements — buttons, list items, navigation |
    | 24px | Feature callouts, card icons, section markers |

    **Colour application:**
    - **Alchemetryx Orange `#ef7025`** — action icons (submit, download, proceed, trigger)
    - **Clarity Teal `#4fa1ab`** — informational icons (status, help, detail indicators)
    - **Deep Navy `#1a1a2e`** — neutral and structural icons (navigation, layout controls, labels)

    Never use more than two icon colours within a single UI component or section. Colour in iconography should carry semantic meaning — if every icon is a different colour, the system collapses into decoration.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Print Icons">
    For print applications, use **outlined or stroke-weight versions** of icons rather than solid fills. Outlined icons reproduce more cleanly across different paper stocks and print methods, where ink spread can cause solid icon fills to appear heavier than intended.

    Maintain a minimum icon size of **8pt** in print. Below this threshold, fine details in icon shapes become illegible, particularly in reproduction methods with lower resolution. At or near the minimum size, always accompany the icon with a text label — never rely on an icon alone to communicate meaning at small print sizes, as recognition rates drop sharply without contextual support.

    When supplying icons for print production, embed them as vector paths within your document rather than linking raster images. This ensures the icon scales cleanly at any reproduction size without quality loss.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Note>
  All photography and illustration used in Alchemetryx materials must have a valid, documented licence or be original work created specifically for the brand. Before using any image in published materials, confirm and record the licence type, the source, the rights holder, and any attribution requirements. Keep this documentation in the brand asset library alongside the file itself. Using unlicensed imagery exposes the organisation to legal and reputational risk — when in doubt, commission original work or use a verified royalty-free source.
</Note>
