The Alchemetryx voice is the spoken expression of the brand — bold enough to be remembered, warm enough to be trusted, and precise enough to be believed. Every word we write, every headline we craft, and every report we deliver should feel unmistakably like us: rigorous without being cold, confident without being arrogant, and always grounded in the real outcomes we create for the people we serve.

## Voice in Practice

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Headlines">
    Use headlines to anchor attention and signal value immediately. A strong Alchemetryx headline is direct, outcome-focused, and earns its reader's curiosity without resorting to clickbait.

    **On-Brand Headlines**

    - "Turn Your Data Into Your Biggest Competitive Asset"
    - "Strategy That's Built on Evidence, Not Assumptions"
    - "Measurable Transformation Starts With the Right Partner"

    **Off-Brand Headlines**

    - "We're Disrupting the Consulting Game Forever!" *(hypey, vague, exclamation mark)*
    - "Unlock Unlimited Growth Potential With Our Synergistic Solutions" *(jargon-heavy, hollow)*
    - "Why Every Business Needs to Pivot to Data-Driven Paradigms Now" *(buzzword overload, alarmist)*
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Social Media">
    On LinkedIn, write as a trusted expert sharing a genuine perspective — not as a brand account pushing content. Lead with a real insight, follow with context, and close with an invitation to engage or learn more.

    **On-Brand LinkedIn Post — Example 1**

    Most strategy projects fail before they start. Not because the thinking is wrong — but because the data informing the thinking is incomplete. At Alchemetryx, we begin every engagement by auditing what you actually know versus what you assume you know. The gap between those two things is usually where the real opportunity hides.

    *What does your data tell you that surprises you? Drop it in the comments.*

    ---

    **On-Brand LinkedIn Post — Example 2**

    Creativity without measurement is decoration. Measurement without creativity is noise. The best-performing organisations we work with have learned to hold both simultaneously — and it's one of the hardest things to build, and one of the most valuable. That's exactly why we exist.

    *We'd love to hear: which side of this balance does your team struggle with most?*
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Email Subject Lines">
    Subject lines should earn the open by being specific, relevant, and honest. Avoid false urgency, vague teases, or anything that feels like a mass marketing blast.

    **On-Brand Subject Line Examples**

    1. "Your Q3 data story — and what it's not telling you"
    2. "Three things we noticed in your brand audit (and one we didn't expect)"
    3. "A framework for turning messy metrics into a clear growth narrative"
    4. "What separates high-performing strategy from expensive activity"
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Proposals & Reports">
    Formal writing is where the Alchemetryx voice must work hardest — maintaining warmth and precision under the weight of complex information. Follow these principles when writing proposals, strategy documents, and client reports.

    **Lead with the client, not yourself.** Open proposals by demonstrating that you understand the client's situation, not by listing your credentials.

    **Use plain language for complex ideas.** If a sentence requires a second read to parse, rewrite it. Complexity in your thinking should not become complexity in your writing.

    **Be specific about outcomes.** Replace phrases like "improved performance" with "a 22% reduction in customer acquisition cost over six months." Numbers build trust.

    **Structure before you write.** Every formal document should have a clear narrative arc: situation, insight, recommendation, outcome. Don't let structure be an afterthought.

    **Attribute and evidence.** When you make a claim, back it with data, a case study, or a logical argument. Never ask the reader to take your word for it.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Grammar & Style

<Accordion title="Punctuation">
  **Oxford comma:** Always use the Oxford comma in lists of three or more items. "Data, strategy, and creativity" — not "data, strategy and creativity." It prevents ambiguity and maintains a clean, professional register.

  **Em dashes:** Use em dashes to add emphasis or introduce a clarifying clause — like this. Do not use hyphens in place of em dashes. Leave a space on either side when used mid-sentence.

  **Exclamation marks:** Use sparingly and intentionally. A single exclamation mark in a long document can land with impact. Three in a paragraph read as noise. When in doubt, leave it out.

  **Ellipses:** Avoid in formal writing. In social or conversational contexts, use them deliberately to create pause — not to trail off without saying something.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Capitalisation">
  **Sentence case for headings:** Write headings as you would a sentence — capitalise only the first word and any proper nouns. "Our approach to data strategy" is correct. "Our Approach To Data Strategy" is not.

  **Proper nouns only:** Capitalise names of specific organisations, people, products, and places. Do not capitalise roles (e.g. "the chief executive"), departments (e.g. "the marketing team"), or general concepts (e.g. "transformation strategy").

  **The Alchemetryx name:** Always written as one word, capital A, lowercase the rest: Alchemetryx. Never ALL CAPS unless in a logo context. Never hyphenated.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Numbers">
  **Spell out one through nine:** Write "three pillars," "seven clients," "two recommendations." Use numerals for 10 and above: "12 months," "47 data points," "200 respondents."

  **Always use numerals for data:** When writing statistics, percentages, monetary values, or measurements, use numerals regardless of size. "A 6% increase," "3 data sources," "$2 million in unlocked value."

  **Percentages:** Use the % symbol in all contexts rather than spelling out "percent."

  **Large numbers:** Use a combination of numerals and words for readability: "1.4 million," "$2.3 billion." Avoid long strings of digits.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Acronyms">
  **Spell out on first use:** Always write the full term followed by its acronym in parentheses the first time it appears in a document. "Key Performance Indicator (KPI)" — after that, KPI alone is acceptable.

  **Avoid acronym overload:** If a document requires more than four or five acronyms, consider whether all of them are necessary. Dense acronym usage reduces readability and excludes new readers.

  **Industry shorthand:** Do not assume your reader shares your fluency. Even common acronyms like ROI, CRM, or SLA should be spelled out in client-facing documents where the audience is mixed.
</Accordion>
