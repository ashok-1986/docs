Alchemetryx reaches ideal clients through a deliberate mix of owned, earned, and paid channels — prioritised by the channels where our ICP is most active and receptive. Channel strategy is not about being everywhere; it is about being consistently excellent in the right places. Before adding a new channel, always ask whether you are fully extracting value from the tier-one channels. Depth in a few channels beats breadth across many.

## Channel Priority Matrix

Use this matrix to allocate time, resource, and budget across channels. Tier 1 channels are non-negotiable — they receive consistent investment regardless of pipeline pressure. Tier 2 channels are important but secondary. Tier 3 channels are tested and scaled only when Tier 1 is performing.

| Channel | Priority | Primary Purpose | Key Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn (Company + Founder)** | Tier 1 | Brand awareness + inbound lead generation | Followers, engagement rate, DM conversations initiated |
| **Referral & Network** | Tier 1 | High-quality warm introductions | Referral conversion rate, referral pipeline value |
| **Content Marketing (Blog + Newsletter)** | Tier 2 | Authority building + organic search | Monthly unique readers, newsletter subscriber growth |
| **Speaking & Events** | Tier 2 | Credibility building + direct ICP access | Events attended, qualified conversations started |
| **Strategic Partnerships** | Tier 2 | Co-selling and reciprocal referral | Partner-sourced pipeline value |
| **Outbound (Email + LinkedIn DM)** | Tier 3 | Direct ICP outreach at scale | Reply rate, discovery calls booked per 100 contacts |
| **Paid Social (LinkedIn Ads)** | Tier 3 | Targeted awareness and retargeting | Cost per lead, diagnostic workshop bookings |

## LinkedIn Playbook

LinkedIn is the single most important channel for Alchemetryx awareness and relationship-building. The founder and senior team profiles are the primary brand voice — they carry more authority and reach than the company page alone. Follow this playbook with consistency.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Post Three Times Per Week — Insight, Story, Opinion">
    Maintain a rhythm of three content types across the week: one post leading with a data point, trend, or market insight relevant to transformation or growth; one post telling a client story, case study moment, or personal experience that illustrates the Alchemetryx approach; and one post sharing a direct opinion or contrarian perspective on strategy, consulting, or brand. Consistency is more important than perfection — publish on a schedule and improve over time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Engage in ICP Comments Daily">
    Identify ten to fifteen accounts in your ideal client segment and spend fifteen minutes per day engaging meaningfully in their content. Leave substantive comments — add a data point, challenge an assumption, or share a relevant experience. Visible, intelligent engagement builds familiarity before any direct outreach and signals that Alchemetryx is plugged into the conversation that matters to your prospects.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send Five Personalised Connection Requests Per Day to ICP Targets">
    Build the network with intention, not volume. Each connection request should reference something specific — a post they published, a mutual connection, a shared event, or a comment they made. Generic connection requests are ignored. A personalised request that demonstrates genuine interest in the person's work converts at a meaningfully higher rate and sets the right tone for the relationship that follows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish One Long-Form Article Per Month">
    Monthly long-form articles — 800 to 1,500 words — position Alchemetryx as a genuine authority on transformation, strategy, and growth. Articles should address a real problem your ICP faces, offer a structured perspective or framework, and close with a practical implication. They perform as evergreen assets, appearing in search, being shared by connections, and being referenced in sales conversations as evidence of expertise.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Track DM Conversations Weekly and Follow Up Consistently">
    LinkedIn direct message conversations are where relationships become opportunities. Review active conversations weekly: who has responded but not progressed? Who attended a recent event or liked a post and could be engaged with a value-add message? The follow-up is where most salespeople fall short — set a weekly calendar block to review and move every relevant conversation forward with a useful, non-pushy message.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Outbound Sequence

When direct outreach is appropriate — for warm ICP targets who have not yet engaged organically — use these structured sequences. Both sequences are built on the principle of leading with value before making any ask.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Email Sequence">
    **Touch 1 — Value-First Introduction (Day 1)**
    Subject: [Specific insight relevant to their business context]
    Open with a genuine observation about their business, market, or a challenge their sector is facing. Share one useful insight or framework — no pitch, no ask. Close with a low-friction offer: *"Happy to share the full framework if it's useful — no call required."*

    **Touch 2 — Relevant Proof Point (Day 5–7)**
    Follow up with a brief case study or result that directly maps to a challenge you inferred from your research. One paragraph. Keep it specific and commercial: *"We helped a similar business in [sector] [achieve specific outcome] in [timeframe] — the approach might be relevant to what you're navigating."*

    **Touch 3 — Direct Ask With Easy Exit (Day 12–14)**
    A short, direct message asking for a twenty-minute conversation. Acknowledge this is your final follow-up. Give them a clear, low-friction out: *"If the timing isn't right, no problem — I'll stay in touch via content and we can reconnect when it makes sense."* Never pressure, never guilt. Leave the door permanently open.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="LinkedIn DM Sequence">
    **Touch 1 — Personalised Connection + Context (Day 1)**
    Request to connect with a short, specific note referencing something genuinely relevant: their content, a shared event, a mutual connection's recommendation, or a specific challenge their business appears to be navigating. No pitch. The goal is to earn the connection, not open a sales conversation.

    **Touch 2 — Insight Drop After Connection (Day 3–5)**
    Once connected, send a brief message sharing something useful — a piece of Alchemetryx content, a relevant data point, or a framework that maps to their context. Frame it explicitly as genuinely useful, not as an introduction to a sales conversation: *"Thought this might be relevant given what you're building — no agenda, just sharing something that's been useful with similar teams."*

    **Touch 3 — Soft Invitation (Day 10–14)**
    Invite them to a low-commitment touchpoint: a diagnostic workshop, a webinar, a piece of exclusive content, or simply a brief conversation. Make the ask feel like an offer: *"If you'd ever find it useful to pressure-test your current positioning with a fresh set of eyes, I'd be happy to spend thirty minutes on it — no cost, no obligation."*
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Partnership Types

Strategic partnerships extend Alchemetryx reach into warm networks without the cost of paid acquisition. Each partnership type works differently — choose partners whose client base overlaps with the Alchemetryx ICP without competing on services.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Complementary Agencies" icon="handshake" href="#channel-priority-matrix">
    Technology implementation agencies, PR firms, and digital marketing studios that serve the same mid-market B2B client base but do not provide strategy or brand services. The mutual value exchange is straightforward: they refer clients who need strategic foundation, and Alchemetryx refers clients who need execution services outside our scope. Formalise these relationships with a simple referral agreement and a shared introduction process.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Technology Vendors" icon="microchip" href="#channel-priority-matrix">
    CRM, analytics, and marketing technology vendors whose products are most valuable when deployed on a sound strategic foundation. Alchemetryx partners with relevant vendors to provide strategic context for their tools — and vendors refer clients who are purchasing technology without the strategy to use it effectively. Co-produced content (webinars, guides) is a natural collaboration vehicle for this partnership type.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Industry Associations" icon="building-columns" href="#channel-priority-matrix">
    Sector-specific trade associations and professional bodies whose membership includes Alchemetryx ideal clients. The value exchange: Alchemetryx provides thought leadership content, speaks at association events, and contributes expertise — in return gaining visibility and implicit endorsement among a concentrated group of qualified prospects. Association partnerships build credibility through association (a deliberate double meaning).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Executive Networks" icon="user-tie" href="#channel-priority-matrix">
    CEO, CMO, and CDO peer networks and roundtable groups that convene senior decision-makers from mid-market organisations. Alchemetryx seeks membership, speaking slots, and facilitation roles within these networks — not to pitch, but to demonstrate expertise in a context where trust transfers rapidly between peers. A warm introduction from a respected peer in an executive network is worth twenty cold outreach messages.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
Focus 80% of GTM energy on Tier 1 channels first. Before investing time in events, partnerships, or paid media, ensure LinkedIn and referral activity is consistent, disciplined, and measured. Do not spread across all channels simultaneously — depth beats breadth, and a single well-executed channel compounds faster than five poorly executed ones.
</Tip>
