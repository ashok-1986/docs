Buyers rarely choose between Alchemetryx and one named competitor. Most owners have never heard of us, and that is fine. What they recognise is a familiar pain: a process that still depends on a person showing up, and a hope that AI or automation might fix it. Their real decision is which category of provider they trust with that hope. This page maps those categories so our team knows where we win, where we lose, and what to say when a buyer compares us to the usual alternatives.

## Alternative categories the buyer considers

| Category | What they offer | Where they win | Where they lose vs Alchemetryx |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI or automation agencies | Sell more tools, more licenses, more integrations. Monthly retainers tied to platform spend. | Buyers who want a fast demo or a shiny interface. Scale messaging. | They rarely measure what changed after the tool went in. Scope creeps. The buyer ends up with more software and the same manual steps. |
| Generalist management consultants | Sell large "transformation" programmes. Strategy decks. Multi-phase roadmaps. | Board credibility. Brand names. Complex programmes that need dozens of hours of discovery. | Programmes take quarters or years. The owner still has no working process at day 90. Price balloons. |
| IT support or MSPs | Keep systems running. Patch, monitor, reset passwords. Break-fix. | Trusted for uptime and security. Already on the supplier list. | They do not rebuild processes. They stabilise what exists. AI adoption is not their day job. |
| In-house DIY | Owner or ops manager reads articles, trials Copilot, builds a spreadsheet, writes a Zapier. | Free. No procurement. Instant start. | No time to finish. No measurement. No independence when the one person leaves. The process still breaks when they are on holiday. |
| Doing nothing or status quo | No spend. No risk. No change. | Zero upfront cost. No vendor management. | The competitive cost of the status quo is usually understated: lost hours, errors, staff turnover, delayed customer response. |

## Where we win

- **Fixed price for a fixed scope.** Our "One Process Rebuild" is £5,950 ex VAT. You know the cost before kick-off.
- **One process at a time.** We do not sell roadmaps. We rebuild invoicing, or quoting, or rota scheduling. One running process in 3–4 weeks.
- **Evidence in a single page.** The Decision Brief states what is working, what is not, what was considered, what we advise, and what happens if you do nothing.
- **Founder to founder.** Ashok speaks with owners, CEOs, and COOs. No account manager hand-off.
- **Honest when doing nothing is better.** If the numbers do not support a rebuild, we say so.
- **Real proofs you can verify.** Care Rota (cleared for public reference), Diversity Festival (documented), and Fitosys (our own build, labelled as such).

## Where we lose (and that is OK)

- **Multi-process "transformation" programmes.** If a buyer wants a twelve-month roadmap across six departments before anything runs, we are not the right fit.
- **Commodity IT support.** We do not monitor servers or reset passwords. MSPs win here.
- **Buyers who want rate cards or day rates.** We scope to outcomes and fixed prices. Day-rate engagements create scope creep.
- **Buyers unwilling to be a case study.** Our growth model depends on honest, named proof. If a firm cannot be referenced, we may decline or adjust terms.

## Handling comparisons in conversation

Use these patterns when a buyer mentions an alternative they are considering.

- "They sell tools. We measure what your existing tools returned before recommending anything else."
- "A twelve-month roadmap sounds reassuring. We prefer one process running without you in four weeks. Then you decide if the next process is worth it."
- "If your MSP had rebuilt the process already, you would not be on this call. We respect what they do. We do what they do not."
- "Doing it yourself is brave. Most owners we meet tried it and ran out of runway. We finish the build and hand you a measured result."
- "We are not cheaper than doing nothing. We are cheaper than a second failed pilot."

## What we do NOT position against

These approaches are not our competitors, and we do not speak ill of them.

- Individual freelancers or contractors doing good work
- Universities, government programmes, or regional business support
- Software vendors selling direct to SMEs
- Peer networking groups or masterminds
- Competent in-house teams who have already solved the problem

Our posture is observation, not attack. If a buyer has found a good solution, we say so.
