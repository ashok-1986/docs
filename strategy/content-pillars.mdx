Alchemetryx content exists to demonstrate expertise, build trust, and attract ideal clients — not to generate likes. Every piece of content should leave a reader better informed and more aware of the transformation Alchemetryx delivers. Content that entertains without educating is marketing noise. Content that educates without connecting back to a clear strategic perspective is forgettable. The standard is simple: would an ambitious mid-market leader read this and think *these people understand my world better than I do*? If the answer is yes, publish it. If not, go deeper.

## Three Content Pillars

Every piece of Alchemetryx content belongs to one of three pillars. Before creating anything, identify which pillar it serves — and ensure each pillar is receiving consistent investment across the editorial calendar.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Transformation Insights" icon="lightbulb" href="#content-calendar-structure">
    Data, research, and original thinking about business transformation, strategy, and growth. This is the authority pillar — it establishes Alchemetryx as the leading voice on organisational transformation for mid-market B2B leaders. Content in this pillar challenges conventional thinking, introduces new frameworks, and connects external trends to the specific challenges our ICP faces. It should feel like reading research from a firm that has done the thinking you haven't had time to do.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Client Success Stories" icon="trophy" href="#content-calendar-structure">
    How Alchemetryx has helped real clients achieve measurable results. This is the proof point pillar — it converts existing interest into trust by demonstrating that the transformation we describe is real and repeatable. Client stories should always be grounded in commercial outcomes: not *"we refreshed their brand"* but *"within six months of repositioning, they closed three enterprise contracts in a market segment they had previously been invisible in."* Specificity is credibility.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tools & Frameworks" icon="wrench" href="#content-calendar-structure">
    Practical models, templates, and frameworks that ICP clients can apply immediately — without hiring Alchemetryx first. This is the generosity pillar. By giving away genuinely useful intellectual property, Alchemetryx demonstrates both the quality of our thinking and a confidence that sharing our tools will only deepen client desire to work with us, not diminish it. The best frameworks double as sales tools: a prospect who has applied the Alchemetryx positioning framework already trusts the methodology before the first call.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Content Formats

Alchemetryx content appears across four primary formats. Each has a distinct purpose, audience mindset, and production standard. Match the right content to the right format rather than defaulting to one medium for everything.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Articles & Blog Posts">
    **Purpose:** Long-form authority building and organic search visibility.

    **Length:** 800–2,000 words. Shorter pieces lack depth; longer pieces require exceptional structuring to retain attention.

    **Frequency:** Minimum one per fortnight. Consistent publication builds domain authority and signals an active, credible organisation.

    **Structure:** Open with the most important insight — do not bury the lead. Use subheadings to make the article scannable. Close with a specific, actionable implication for the reader. Every article should include at least one original framework, data point, or perspective that cannot be found elsewhere.

    **SEO:** Each article should target a specific keyword or question that an Alchemetryx ideal client would search. Optimise the title, meta description, and first 150 words without compromising readability. Prioritise topics where Alchemetryx can genuinely own a distinctive perspective — not topics where the content will be indistinguishable from a thousand other results.

    **Distribution:** Publish to the Alchemetryx blog, share a summary on LinkedIn with a link, and include in the monthly newsletter. Strong articles should also be submitted for syndication on relevant industry publications.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="LinkedIn Posts">
    **Purpose:** Awareness, engagement, and relationship-building with ICP audiences.

    **Length:** Short form (150–300 words) for punchy insight or opinion. Long form (600–1,200 words) for structured frameworks, stories, or deeper analysis.

    **Frequency:** Three posts per week. Rotate across content types: insight, story, and opinion. Consistency of schedule trains the algorithm and builds audience expectation.

    **Format principles:** Open with a hook that earns the *see more* click — a surprising statistic, a counterintuitive claim, or a direct challenge to a common assumption. Use short paragraphs and white space — dense blocks of text are ignored on mobile. Close with a clear point of view or a question that invites genuine response.

    **What to avoid:** Vague motivational content. Engagement bait (e.g., *"comment YES if you agree"*). Posts that announce rather than teach. Any post that could have been written by anyone in any industry — Alchemetryx content must be specific, opinionated, and recognisably ours.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Newsletter">
    **Purpose:** Deepening relationships with a warm, opted-in audience and maintaining top-of-mind presence with potential and current clients.

    **Frequency:** Monthly. Consistency matters more than frequency — a reliable monthly newsletter builds habit and anticipation.

    **Structure:** Every edition follows the same three-part format:
    1. **One big insight** — a data point, trend, or perspective that reframes how readers think about transformation, strategy, or growth. 200–300 words.
    2. **One framework** — a practical tool readers can apply immediately, drawn from Alchemetryx methodology or current client work. 150–200 words.
    3. **One client story** — a brief, outcome-focused story illustrating the transformation Alchemetryx delivered. 100–200 words. Client identity can be anonymised where necessary.

    **Tone:** Warmer and more personal than a blog post. The newsletter is a direct relationship — write as if addressing a respected colleague, not an anonymous audience. The editor's voice should be consistent and distinctive.

    **Subject lines:** Specific, curiosity-driven, and short (under 50 characters). Never clickbait. The subject line is a promise — the content must deliver on it.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Case Studies">
    **Purpose:** Converting interest into trust by demonstrating specific, measurable transformation outcomes.

    **Length:** 500–1,000 words. Long enough to tell a credible story; short enough to be read in full.

    **Structure:** Every Alchemetryx case study follows the same four-part format, without exception:
    1. **Situation** — Who the client is, what their market context was, and what condition they were in at the start.
    2. **Challenge** — The specific strategic, brand, or data problem that was blocking growth or transformation.
    3. **Approach** — How Alchemetryx diagnosed, designed, and delivered the solution — with enough specificity to demonstrate the quality of the thinking.
    4. **Outcome** — Quantified results wherever possible. Revenue impact, market share, efficiency gains, brand metrics, or capability improvements. Avoid qualitative outcomes unless quantitative data is genuinely unavailable.

    **Publishing:** Case studies live on the Alchemetryx website, are referenced in proposals, and are shared on LinkedIn in condensed form. Always seek client approval before publishing — and use the approval process as an opportunity to deepen the relationship.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Content Calendar Structure

The monthly content calendar ensures consistent representation of all three pillars across formats. Rotate through this structure every four weeks.

| Week | Pillar | Primary Format | Secondary Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Transformation Insights | Long-form LinkedIn post + blog article on the same theme | Share article link in relevant LinkedIn comments; include in next newsletter |
| **Week 2** | Tools & Frameworks | LinkedIn post presenting the framework + downloadable PDF or visual asset | Share in partner networks; follow up with ICP contacts who engage |
| **Week 3** | Client Success Stories | LinkedIn case study post (condensed format) + full case study on website | Reference in active sales conversations; tag client with permission |
| **Week 4** | Transformation Insights | Opinion or thought leadership LinkedIn post + monthly newsletter edition | Engage with all comments; use responses to identify warm prospects |

## Content Quality Checklist

Before publishing any piece of Alchemetryx content — regardless of format or platform — run it through this checklist. Every item must be satisfied.

<Accordion title="Does it reflect one of the three content pillars?">
  If a piece of content cannot be clearly assigned to Transformation Insights, Client Success Stories, or Tools & Frameworks, it should be reworked or discarded. Off-pillar content dilutes brand identity and confuses the audience about what Alchemetryx stands for. If the content has strategic value but doesn't fit the current pillars, use it as evidence that the pillar structure needs reviewing — not that the rule should be ignored.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does it lead with insight rather than self-promotion?">
  The first paragraph, sentence, or hook of any Alchemetryx content must give the reader something before it asks for anything. If the opening is primarily about Alchemetryx — what we do, who we are, how great we are — rewrite it to lead with the insight, challenge, or outcome that makes the reader care. Self-promotional content is scrolled past; insight-led content is read, shared, and remembered.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does it have one clear, specific takeaway?">
  Every piece of content should leave the reader with one thing they can do, think, or see differently as a result of reading it. If you cannot state the takeaway in a single sentence, the content lacks focus. Multiple takeaways dilute impact — choose the strongest one and build everything else around it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the tone on-brand — bold, warm, and specific?">
  Alchemetryx content should sound confident without being arrogant, warm without being casual, and specific without being jargon-heavy. Read it aloud. Does it sound like something a trusted, brilliant colleague would say? Or does it sound like a corporate brochure or an overexcited press release? If the latter, rewrite in plain, direct language that respects the reader's intelligence.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Has it been proofread by a second person?">
  No Alchemetryx content is published without a second pair of eyes. This is not a bureaucratic requirement — it is a brand standard. Typos, grammatical errors, and factual mistakes in published content undermine the credibility that every other investment in quality is designed to build. Build proofreading into the production workflow, not as an afterthought.
</Accordion>

<Note>
Repurpose every long-form piece into at least three LinkedIn posts. A 1,500-word article contains at least three distinct insights, each of which can stand alone as a short-form LinkedIn post. Content efficiency is a competitive advantage — a single well-researched article, properly repurposed, delivers six weeks of LinkedIn content across three posts. Document your repurposing plan before you publish, not after.
</Note>
