Knowing who you serve best is not a limitation — it is a competitive advantage. Alchemetryx is built to deliver transformation for a specific type of organisation: one that is ambitious enough to demand real change, complex enough to need expert guidance, and mature enough to act on what strategy reveals. Defining our Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) means every sales conversation, every marketing decision, and every delivery choice is calibrated toward the clients where we create the most value — and who, in turn, become our most successful long-term partners.

## What is an ICP?

An Ideal Customer Profile is a precise description of the type of organisation that benefits most from working with you. It is built from firmographic data, observed patterns in your best client relationships, and a clear-eyed understanding of where your methodology creates outsized value. Unlike a target audience defined purely by demographics, an ICP describes organisational characteristics — size, sector, growth stage, decision-making structure, and strategic readiness — that predict whether an engagement will succeed.

<Info>
  An Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) describes the type of organisation that gets the most value from your service and is most likely to become a long-term, successful client. It is not a wishlist — it is a strategic filter.
</Info>

When Alchemetryx works with organisations that fit this profile, engagements move faster, outcomes are stronger, and the relationship is more likely to grow. When we work outside this profile, friction is higher and results are harder to achieve. The ICP exists to protect both sides of that equation.

## Alchemetryx ICP at a Glance

The table below captures the core firmographic and situational criteria that define the organisations Alchemetryx is built to serve.

| Dimension | Profile |
|---|---|
| Organisation Size | 50–5,000 employees |
| Annual Revenue | £5M – £500M |
| Geography | UK, Europe, USA (English-speaking) |
| Sector | Professional Services, Technology, Financial Services, Healthcare, Consumer Brands |
| Growth Stage | Scale-up, Growth, or Strategic Transformation phase |
| Decision Maker | CEO, CMO, CCO, VP Strategy, Head of Growth |
| Budget Cycle | Annual planning cycle with discretionary project budget |
| Engagement Type | Project-based (3–12 months) or ongoing retainer |

## Why This Profile?

Organisations in the 50–5,000 employee range occupy a distinctive strategic position. They are large enough to have real budget authority and genuine organisational complexity, but they are rarely large enough to have built the depth of internal strategic, creative, and analytical capability that transformation demands. The Chief Executive has a vision but no roadmap. The marketing team is executing but not strategising. The data is accumulating but not becoming decisions.

This is exactly where Alchemetryx adds the most value. We step into the gap between ambition and capability — bringing the strategic rigour, creative depth, and data fluency that these organisations need but cannot cost-effectively hire full-time. The revenue range of £5M–£500M reflects organisations with enough commercial maturity to invest seriously in transformation and enough at stake to demand measurable outcomes. The sectors we focus on — Professional Services, Technology, Financial Services, Healthcare, and Consumer Brands — are all industries experiencing significant pressure to transform, differentiate, and grow in environments where the rules are changing fast.

## ICP vs. Non-ICP

Use these signals to qualify opportunities quickly. Strong ICP fit does not guarantee a win, but weak ICP fit almost always predicts a difficult engagement.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Strong ICP Fit" icon="check">
    - The organisation has a named senior sponsor (CEO, CMO, or equivalent) who owns the transformation agenda
    - They have attempted strategic change before and understand why external expertise accelerates it
    - A discretionary project budget exists or is actively being sought for this engagement
    - The organisation faces a clear external or internal trigger — competitive pressure, a growth milestone, a brand or data challenge — that creates urgency
    - They are asking strategic questions ("Where should we grow?" "What should our brand stand for?") rather than purely executional ones ("Can you run our ads?")
  </Card>
  <Card title="Weak ICP Fit" icon="xmark">
    - The organisation has fewer than 50 employees and no dedicated strategic budget — transformation is a luxury, not a priority
    - The engagement request is purely transactional: deliverable-for-fee with no interest in strategic outcomes or partnership
    - There is no senior decision-maker engaged — the conversation is stuck at a mid-level manager with no budget authority
    - The organisation needs commodity services — templated output, low-cost production, or volume execution — rather than bespoke strategy
    - Leadership is not aligned: competing internal factions mean no strategic initiative will get the buy-in it needs to land
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
